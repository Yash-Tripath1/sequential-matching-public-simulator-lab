A Reported +0.175 Advantage Does Not Survive Twelve Times the Data

A two-phase non-replication study in sequential reciprocal matching

Abstract

Sequential reciprocal matching is a daily allocation problem under uncertainty. The outcome of interest, mutual second-meeting intention per 100 arrived members, is rare — roughly one qualifying event per episode. That makes it too noisy to separate competent policies at any sample size a single research team can afford.

Phase 1 of this work screened seventeen policy configurations on ten public seeds and reported that one beat the supplied greedy baseline by +0.175 MSMI/100 (95% seed-block CI [+0.092, +0.258], p = 0.016). Phase 2 recovered that configuration from surviving code, confirmed it reproduces the published headline exactly — 0.525 and 63 events, matching on 60 of 60 episodes against the stored per-episode record — and re-tested it on 120 fresh seeds that were never used in Phase 1.

The finding

Did not replicate The Phase 1 advantage. The two estimates are 7.09 standard errors apart, and their confidence intervals do not overlap. No arm beat the supplied baseline at 120 seeds.

Did replicate The structural findings. Clarification is essential (−0.304 when removed, p < 0.001), and coverage saturates at 97.8% of its structural ceiling, with two independent computations of that ceiling agreeing within 3%. Findings about structure survive; findings about ranking do not.

Why Three independent lines: the score is supply-bound; 64% of the variance in the acceptance outcome is unobservable by construction; and the instrument rests on roughly 104 events. A screen whose resolution exceeds the spread it is measuring returns an artefact as its winner.
